When 90% isn't 90%

A conformal prediction interval promises to contain the true value 90% of the time. The promise holds if the data is exchangeable, which roughly means its order doesn't matter. Hourly electricity demand doesn't meet that condition. I trained a model on early-2020 Texas grid data, calibrated its intervals once on late 2020, and let it predict through 2024. Move the slider, change how the interval is calibrated, and see what survives.

Why it breaks

Two separate problems, and recalibrating only touches one of them.

1. The model falls behind

Average gap between real demand and the model's prediction. It was trained on 2020 only, so as Texas demand grew the predictions stayed put.

2. The seasons differ

Coverage averaged by calendar month. The interval was sized on autumn errors, and the summer errors are bigger.

The model is gradient boosting on calendar features only (hour, weekday, month, weekend) with no weather input. That makes it a weak forecaster, which is why the intervals are so wide. Conformal prediction guarantees validity under its assumptions, not usefulness. Monthly recalibration is a heuristic and carries no coverage guarantee of its own.
